### What market is Global AI really trying to win?

The company is not competing for the general cloud computing market. It is targeting a specific and rapidly separating sub-market: compute environments where the organization deploying AI cannot accept multi-tenancy, shared infrastructure, foreign data jurisdiction, or hyperscaler lock-in.

This is the sovereign AI infrastructure market, and it is structurally different from the broader cloud market in three ways:

1. The customer cannot use public cloud by choice, regulation, or national policy. This includes defense contractors, intelligence-adjacent government agencies, large financial institutions, energy utilities, healthcare systems, and nation-state AI initiatives.

2. The customer needs the most advanced GPU systems on the planet (GB300 NVL72, Grace Blackwell) at densities and thermal profiles that only 5 percent of existing data centers globally can support.

3. The customer needs single-tenant isolation, air-gapping, and data sovereignty controls that are structurally incompatible with AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud's shared-infrastructure model.

Global AI's thesis is that this is not a niche. It is the next wave of enterprise and government AI adoption, and the hyperscalers cannot serve it by design.

## SECTION E — INFRASTRUCTURE STRATEGY

**What Global AI is actually building.**

### The Off-Prem Model (Trademarked)

Global AI coined the term "Off-Prem" as a commercial and architectural concept. The premise is that the customer gets the security, control, and isolation of on-premises infrastructure without the capital expenditure, facilities management, power procurement, cooling engineering, or NVIDIA relationship required to build it themselves. It is effectively a white-glove managed compute service with full single-tenant isolation.

The architectural differentiation from standard neocloud:

| Standard Neocloud (CoreWeave, Lambda, Nebius) | Global AI Off-Prem |
|---|---|
| Multi-tenant or logical separation | Physical single-tenant isolation |
| Shared networking fabric possible | Air-gapped; bare metal and software layer never shared |
| Standard cooling (mostly air) | 100% liquid cooling: Direct-to-Chip + Rear Door Heat Exchangers |
| Self-service, developer-focused | Enterprise and government white-glove deployment |
| Public cloud adjacency | No public internet exposure by design |

### Hardware Stack

**NVIDIA GB200 NVL72:** Already deployed at Endicott. 72 Grace Blackwell GPUs per rack-scale system. The NVL72 configuration is rack-scale supercomputing — not individual GPU deployment. This is the hardware floor of their current deployment.

**NVIDIA GB300 NVL72:** First shipment installed at Endicott as of December 2025. Company claims this is the largest deployment of GB300s in New York State. GB300 is NVIDIA's next-generation Blackwell Ultra configuration with increased memory bandwidth and improved thermal profile — designed for the most demanding LLM training and inference workloads.

**NVIDIA Quantum-X800 InfiniBand:** The networking fabric connecting GPU clusters. This is not commodity Ethernet. X800 InfiniBand is NVIDIA's highest-performance AI interconnect, required for the communication throughput needed in distributed training across NVL72 systems.

**Liquid cooling availability:** The company states that liquid-cooling capable infrastructure is available in only 5 percent of data centers globally. This is a defensible claim. GB300 NVL72 systems require active liquid cooling to operate at sustained performance levels. Most existing colocation and data center infrastructure cannot physically support this hardware without a full facility redesign.

### Footprint

**Endicott, New York:** 32MW operational. Two buildings at 1701 North Street. Site was originally built by IBM. Both founders have IBM lineage. The IBM birthplace connection is not incidental — it is likely how the founders had pre-existing relationships with the site and regional power infrastructure.

**Windsor / Weld County, Colorado:** 438 acres acquired for $15.6M from a developer who had purchased the former Carestream Health (spun from Eastman Kodak) campus. Initial phase targeting 18-24MW by end of 2026. Expandable to 60MW in medium term. Company cites a 1GW aspirational target for the full site. Power discussions underway with Poudre Valley Rural Electric Association and Xcel Energy. No formal county proposal filed as of March 2026. Investment range disclosed: $2 billion to $20 billion depending on final scale.

## SECTION F — COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**NorthStar V3 Structural applied. Stakeholder mapping complete.**

### The Four-Box Competitive Framework

**Box 1 — Hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP)**
These are the structural competitors Global AI is positioning against. The hyperscaler model is multi-tenant, shared-infrastructure, and cannot offer physical air-gapping or single-tenant isolation at the architecture level without building a fundamentally different product. Hyperscalers have announced sovereign cloud regions (AWS GovCloud, Azure Government, Google Public Sector), but these are logically isolated — not physically air-gapped. They serve compliance postures, not true sovereignty. This is the primary white space Global AI occupies.

**Box 2 — Standard Neoclouds (CoreWeave, Lambda Labs, Nebius, Nscale, Crusoe)**
These are GPU cloud providers optimized for developer-facing AI workloads. CoreWeave is the largest, with 250,000+ GPUs across 43 data centers and contracts with Meta, OpenAI, and Microsoft. Lambda Labs focuses on self-service and developer pricing. Nebius (former Yandex international) targets European sovereign workloads. None of them lead with physical single-tenant air-gapping as the core value proposition. CoreWeave is the most direct competitor at scale, but it serves a different buyer persona: AI model builders and frontier labs, not enterprises with national security or regulatory constraints.

**Box 3 — Sovereign AI Infrastructure Peers (Emerging)**
A small and early peer set: companies like G42 (UAE), HUMAIN itself (though now a partner), and European sovereign compute initiatives. Global AI's differentiator against these is U.S.-based physical infrastructure with NVIDIA partner access and American data jurisdiction.

**Box 4 — Traditional Colocation (Equinix, Digital Realty)**
These operators own the buildings but do not own the GPU fleets, manage the AI infrastructure, or offer the stack integration. They are potential landlords or partners, not direct competitors.

1. **Requirements translation:** Enterprise and government customers arrive with compliance mandates, security requirements, and AI workload specifications. 5. **Governance and compliance mapping:** Government customers will have FedRAMP, CMMC, ITAR, and data residency requirements. Enterprises will have industry-specific regulatory postures. The BA maps infrastructure capabilities to those requirements — this is where governance-layer analysis creates direct commercial value.

6. **Internal documentation and process architecture:** A company growing from 32MW to 1GW needs documented intake processes, customer onboarding workflows, deployment scope frameworks, and change order controls. The BA builds and maintains those artifacts.

## SECTION I — RED FLAGS AND UNKNOWNS

**EBT Deviation Index and NorthStar uncertainty flags applied.**

### Red Flags (Validate Before Accepting an Offer)

1. **No disclosed revenue or customer base.** As of May 2026, Global AI has not publicly named a single paying enterprise or government customer beyond the HUMAIN partnership, which is a deployment agreement rather than a revenue-generating contract. A company that is 18 months old, building at this scale, with this much capital deployment, should have some revenue signal by now. Ask directly: "Who is your anchor customer today?"

2. **Funding amount is undisclosed.** NVIDIA participated in a funding round, but the amount, total raise, and valuation have not been publicly disclosed. This is common at early-stage companies, but it creates uncertainty about runway. Ask: "What is the company's capital position and burn rate relative to the Colorado buildout?"

3. **Colorado site has no formal permit or proposal.** As of March 2026, Weld County's planning office confirmed no formal proposal had been submitted. The acquisition is real, but the development timeline is still conceptual. An 18-24MW initial target by end of 2026 is aggressive given this status.

4. **"World's first Sovereign AI Hyperscaler" is a marketing claim.** The term "hyperscaler" technically refers to companies operating at AWS/Azure/Google scale — typically 1GW+ of operational capacity. Global AI is at 32MW operational today. The branding is forward-looking and aspirational, not descriptive of current scale. Understand the distinction.

5. **HUMAIN is a Saudi PIF vehicle.** HUMAIN's technology and capital commitments are genuine. But HUMAIN is chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and operates within Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 geopolitical framework. The U.S. regulatory environment for Saudi sovereign investment in American AI infrastructure may shift, depending on the administration's approach to foreign-owned compute. This is a low-probability but non-zero risk to the partnership's U.S. deployment plans.

6. **Thin public team for stated ambition.** The careers page as of May 2026 lists a small number of open roles. A company targeting 100MW by year-end with a $2-20B Colorado buildout should be hiring aggressively. If headcount growth does not match capital deployment ambition, that is a signal about either pacing or organizational gaps worth understanding.
